# Yankee Institute

## Register

brand

## Users

This website serves people seeking Yankee Institute's Connecticut policy reporting, research and public resources. Its existing content supports residents and policy readers, journalists, policymakers, event visitors and prospective supporters. These are evidence-based working audience groups; their priority has not been approved by the client.

Editors need to publish timely articles, research, events and campaigns through maintainable WordPress templates. Readers may arrive directly on an article through search, social media or a referring publication, so the homepage cannot be the only clear entry point.

## Product Purpose

Redesign Yankee Institute's existing WordPress website using Elementor in place of Themify. Make policy work easier to find, show that the organization is active, and make relevant next actions apparent. The supplied project brief identifies confusing navigation, buried calls to action and dated presentation.

This is a full website redesign. Empower Mississippi is the process reference for improving an established WordPress site. Its branding is not a source for Yankee's identity. The existing Yankee logo, content, visual tokens and source material are the identity baseline.

## Brand Personality

Working interpretation for design review: authoritative, accessible and active. These words describe the intended response to the supplied brief, not a client-approved brand platform.

Authority comes from clear sourcing, author attribution, dates and usable research. Accessibility comes from plain labels and readable pages. Activity comes from timely content, visible priorities and current events, not decorative motion. Preserve the organization's supplied positions and editorial voice without inventing claims or slogans.

## Anti-references

- Navigation that presents policy topics, content formats and individual programs as interchangeable categories.
- A static-looking homepage that conceals recent work or makes the next action difficult to find.
- Generic campaign or software marketing layouts that replace substantive content with slogans and decorative statistics.
- Another client's fonts, palette, audience, membership structure or homepage decisions.

These are project problem statements and design guardrails, not a claim that the client has approved a particular alternative.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with useful, current work. Let actual reporting, research and events establish relevance.
2. Organize around reader intent. Distinguish topics, formats and organizational information; validate the final taxonomy before migration.
3. Provide a relevant next step. Connect reading to related research, subscriptions, events or support where appropriate. The global CTA priority remains an open business decision.
4. Preserve trust through the redesign. Retain content meaning, attribution, important URLs and required public tools.
5. Make maintenance practical. Prefer reusable templates and explicit editorial controls over layouts that require a developer for routine updates.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA practices with keyboard access, visible focus, readable text, meaningful link labels and reduced-motion support. Do not claim certification. Specific audience accommodations have not been supplied. Design for readers moving between articles, archives and public resources on both phones and larger screens.

## Scope and decisions still required

The prepared Yankee agreement describes structure/design, templates, performance, search, measurement, SEO/AEO/accessibility, launch and handover. Signature status and detailed acceptance criteria remain recorded separately in private project memory.

Confirm audience priorities, primary CTAs, the sitemap, template coverage, brand changes and the retain/rebuild/retire inventory. Existing forms, donations, events, contracts data, charts and calculators need explicit migration decisions. A design document does not authorize retiring content or changing integrations.
